## 1. Executive Summary

Students in Algeria need extra income, but their availability changes with their university timetable and few jobs fit around it. Employers such as cafés, shops and event organisers need extra people at short notice and have no fast way to reach those who are available. Today flexible work is advertised mostly in Facebook and Instagram groups, where offers rarely state pay, schedule or location, cannot be filtered, and carry no recourse if the job is not as described.

DebrJournya is an Android application that matches students with flexible work using their weekly availability. A student builds a profile, a skills list and an availability calendar once, then receives matches or searches and filters listings by location, schedule, pay and type of work. Employers post part-time jobs, single shifts and volunteering or experience events, and accept applicants ranked by fit. The app also builds the student’s CV from their profile and completed missions. We build for Algeria first, in Arabic and French, and launch at five campuses: the Pôle technologique de Sidi Abdellah, ESI, USTHB, Ziania and the Koléa pole (which lies in Tipaza wilaya, not Algiers). Other cities open after the first year. Version 1 serves students only.

The model is freemium. The app is free for both sides, and free users see advertising. Premium removes advertising for students and employers. Student Premium adds early access to new posts (6 hours before other students), a CV customised for a specific application and a profile highlighted to employers. Employer Premium adds unlimited postings, a verified badge, suggested candidates, priority support and boosted posts. Premium and advertising switch on only after the launch liquidity target is met: 60% of posts with pay filled within 48 hours, held for four weeks in Algiers. We forecast 340,000 DZD of revenue in Year 1, 4,845,750 DZD in Year 2 and 17,174,000 DZD in Year 3, and we need 610,000 DZD to cover the first year.

## 2. Mission, Problem and Vision

### 2.1 Problem statement

Students need extra income, but their availability depends on their university schedule, which changes through the year. Few jobs offer the flexibility to fit around it. On the other side, employers who need staff for a shift or an event cannot easily reach available people at short notice. Offers are scattered across social media groups, pages and personal contacts, so neither side can find the other quickly.

### 2.2 Why the problem matters

- **No open channel built for short work.** Existing platforms either target qualified recruitment (Emploitic), list only partner companies (Campuce Jobs), or show no evidence of traction (Worky.dz). See section 4.

- **Information is unstructured.** Offers on Facebook and Instagram do not state pay, schedule or location consistently, and cannot be filtered, so seekers waste time on unsuitable options.

- **Trust.** Informal offers carry a risk of scams, unpaid work presented as paid, and no recourse. The Ministry of Labour warned job seekers in January 2022 against misleading job ads on Facebook. In August 2025 the Banque Extérieure d’Algérie denied a fake remote-recruitment offer circulating on social media, and in June 2026 the security services appealed to victims of fake job offers posted on Facebook (section 14).

- **Lost opportunity for both sides.** Students miss income and experience. Employers lose time or go understaffed.

- **Field-of-study mismatch.** Students are often limited to work in their own field, though many flexible jobs need no specific degree.

- **Experience is hard to find.** Volunteering and event opportunities that build a student’s experience are shared the same informal way and are easy to miss.

### 2.3 Who faces it

Students in higher education (1,884,839 in 2026-27, all cycles) who need income or experience around their timetable. Employers: small shops, cafés, restaurants, event organisers, associations and startups that need flexible staff. Graduates and other young job seekers face the same problem, but version 1 does not serve them (5.3).

### 2.4 Mission statement

**To make finding and filling flexible work simple, fast and trustworthy, so any student can find work that fits their schedule, and any employer can find the right person for a shift.**

### 2.5 Vision

Within three to five years we want DebrJournya to be the default platform for flexible work for students across Algeria, starting around Algiers and expanding city by city. It would grow from a listings board into a trusted network, with ratings, verified employers and a work and experience history that students can show to future employers.

The Year-3 target of 100,000 registered students (5.3% of all students) is the one figure the financial plan depends on. Our other longer-term targets, [X] shifts filled per month and formal partnerships with at least the five launch campuses, are ambitions rather than forecasts. None of them is assumed in the revenue figures in section 7, and each needs a measurement method before we can claim it.

### 4.2 Positioning

Facebook and Instagram groups remain where most flexible work in Algiers is advertised. Offers there rarely state pay, schedule or location in a consistent way, cannot be filtered, and carry no recourse if the job is not as described. That gap is the reason DebrJournya exists, and no platform has closed it yet.

The gap is no longer unoccupied, and the competitive picture changed in 2026. Campuce Jobs is the most serious threat. It targets students directly and has distribution we cannot match: a programme launched with Algérie Poste in November 2025, a partnership with the Higher Education ministry signed in April 2026, and a free student card. Its limits are structural. It lists only partner companies, its visible offers lean toward internships rather than shifts, and access appears tied to the Student Campuce programme. Worky.dz is aimed at the same cafés, restaurants and event organisers we are, and claims verification, matching and secured payment, but we could not find evidence of its traction. Emploitic is the established national job board with a large audience, but it is built for qualified recruitment, and its 2.2 app rating suggests the mobile experience is a weak point.

None of the three combines four things that DebrJournya is built around. First, a weekly availability calendar with date exceptions, which feeds matching directly. Second, single shifts, events and unpaid volunteering in one place, so a student can also build experience. Third, a CV that fills itself from completed work. Fourth, an open marketplace where any employer can post, not a closed partner list. That combination is harder to copy than any single feature, particularly for a platform whose offers come from a fixed partner network.

Trust is where we make the smallest claim. Worky and Campuce Jobs already advertise verified employers, so a verified badge puts us level with them and does not set us apart. We treat it as a requirement to meet, not a differentiator.

## 5. Product and Functionalities

### 5.1 Product overview

DebrJournya is an Android application that matches students with flexible work using their weekly availability. Students build a profile, skills and availability calendar once. Employers post jobs, shifts and events, then accept applicants ranked by fit. After an acceptance, both sides see each other’s phone number and continue by call or WhatsApp. Free users see advertising, and Premium removes it (7.1).

The application is not an employer and does not handle wages. It carries information between the student and the employer, and pay is agreed and settled between them. This keeps version 1 out of payroll and keeps the legal question in R2 answerable.

The match score in F5 starts as a fixed rule, not a learned model. Each part is a value between 0 and 1: **match = 0.5 × availability fit + 0.3 × skills fit + 0.2 × distance fit.** Availability fit is the share of the post’s hours that fall in the student’s free slots. Skills fit is the share of required skills the student has. Distance fit falls from 1 at 0 km to 0 at 15 km. These weights are a starting assumption, tuned during the beta.

F13 and F15 are Should because payment is a GO/NO-GO risk (R4) and because Premium and advertising switch on only once the liquidity target in 10.2 is met. Until R4 is answered, the team can activate Premium by hand after a BaridiMob transfer, so the paid phase does not wait for in-app payment.

### 5.4 Technical approach

The server, not the phone, is the source of truth. Every post and application is shared between two people, so the backend holds the state and enforces the rules. The phone keeps a cache, so the feed and the student’s own applications still open with no network.

|**Layer**|**Choice**|**Rationale and constraint**|
|---|---|---|
|**Client**|Flutter, Android first|One codebase, the framework taught in this module. Right-to-left layout built in from the first screen, not added later.|
|**Backend**|Supabase (PostgreSQL)|Relational data fits users, posts, applications and subscriptions. PostGIS handles distance queries. Open source, so it can be self-hosted in Algeria if R3 requires it.|
|**Authentication**|Phone number plus one-time code|Students use phones more than email. Delivery must be tested on Mobilis, Djezzy and Ooredoo. Codes are rate-limited per number and per device so the SMS bill cannot be abused (R10).|
|**Matching**|Score computed on the server per query|The formula in 5.2 runs as a database query. No machine learning in version 1.|
|**Contact reveal**|Enforced by the server|A phone number is returned by the API only for an accepted application. Hiding it in the app alone would be bypassed.|
|**Premium**|Entitlement flags checked by the server|Early access, boosts, post limits and ad-free status are decided by the server, not the app. One server setting switches the paid phase on. Activated by hand until R4 is answered.|
|**Advertising**|Direct sponsors and house ads at launch|No third-party advertising SDK that collects personal data, to stay within R3 and the size budget. Ads are never shown inside the apply flow.|
|**Location**|Commune level for students|Distance is computed from the commune, not the exact position, to limit personal data (R3). Employer addresses are exact.|
|**Notifications**|Firebase Cloud Messaging plus local reminders|Status changes need push. Shift reminders fire from the phone, so they work offline.|
|**CV**|PDF generated on the phone|No personal data goes to a server just to build a CV.|
|**Analytics**|Counts and timings only|No event carries a name, phone number or CV content.|

Two budgets are fixed now because they are expensive to fix later. The installed application stays under 40 MB. Performance is measured on a 2 GB RAM Android 10 phone on a 3G connection, not on a development machine.

## 7. Business Model and Financial Projections

### 7.1 Revenue streams

The model is freemium. The core of the product is free for both sides: a student can always build a profile, search, get matches, apply and receive the contact of an employer who accepts, and an employer can always review applicants. Free users see advertising. Premium removes it and adds the features below. Premium and advertising start only after the launch liquidity target is met (10.2).

## 8. Unit Economics and Funding

### 8.1 What one user is worth

Revenue per user can be measured in several ways and they give very different numbers, so we separate them here.

|**Metric**|**Value**|**How it is obtained**|
|---|---|---|
|**Subscription price, employer**|2,500 DZD a month|List price of Employer Premium.|
|**Subscription price, student**|1,500 DZD a year|List price of Student Premium.|
|**ARPPU, yearly revenue per paying employer**|30,000 DZD|2,500 × 12.|
|**ARPPU, yearly revenue per paying student**|1,500 DZD|List price.|
|**ARPU, yearly revenue per registered account**|165 DZD|17,174,000 ÷ 104,000 registered students and employers in Year 3. This is the figure that matters for acquisition spending, and it is about 180 times smaller than an employer’s yearly price.|
|**Average paying lifetime**|Employer 12.5 months, student 24 months|Employer: 1 ÷ 8% monthly churn, the target in 10.2. Student: half renew each year, an assumption.|
|**CLV, customer lifetime value**|Employer 31,250 DZD, student 3,000 DZD, blended 5,093 DZD|Employer: 2,500 × 12.5. Student: 1,500 × 2. Blended on the Year-3 mix of 240 employers and 3,000 students.|

### 8.2 What one user costs

|**Metric**|**Value**|**Calculation**|
|---|---|---|
|**CAC, blended across all registrations**|75 DZD|400,000 marketing ÷ 5,300 Year-1 registered accounts (5,000 students and 300 employers).|
|**CAC per paying user**|3,478 DZD|400,000 ÷ 115 Year-1 paying users (100 students and 15 employers). Only paying users repay the cost, so this is the figure we plan against.|
|**LTV : CAC ratio**|1.5 : 1|5,093 ÷ 3,478.|
|**CAC payback period**|11.6 months|3,478 ÷ 300.9, the average monthly revenue per paying user in Year 3 ((3,000 × 1,500 ÷ 12 + 240 × 2,500) ÷ 3,240).|

A ratio of 1.5:1 is half the 3:1 benchmark usually treated as the minimum for a subscription business. The reason is structural: in Year 1 only 115 of 5,300 accounts pay, because Premium starts late and the first spend builds the free base that makes Premium sellable. Two things mean the ratio understates the model. Free users earn advertising revenue that is not in the CLV, and acquisition should get cheaper as referrals and ambassadors replace paid campaigns. But the ratio only improves if conversion rises or acquisition costs fall, so we state it as it is. We have four levers, in order of how quickly we could pull them: cut paid acquisition (150,000 DZD); raise employer conversion by offering Premium right after the first accepted hire; lengthen lifetime with annual prepayment; raise prices last.

## 9. Marketing and Growth Strategy

### 9.1 Phased go-to-market

The central problem is the two-sided cold start (R1): students will not join an app with no jobs, and employers will not post to an app with no students. So we seed supply first. We personally bring in the first employers and event organisers, so that every student who joins sees real, current listings. We compete with Facebook and Instagram groups on their own ground, by being present in the same groups and pages, not by advertising against them. Against Campuce Jobs, which has distribution through the Higher Education ministry and Algérie Poste, we do not compete on reach. We go through the same universities (student offices and clubs) and offer what it does not: a marketplace open to any employer, single shifts and a calendar built around the student’s timetable.

On social channels, Instagram and Facebook are the main channels for students and local employers, with Instagram weighted above its raw size because it reaches more women (3.1). TikTok gives short, shareable reach, YouTube carries the explainer video, and LinkedIn is for employers, associations and startups, where it also adds credibility. Each phase has an exit condition and we do not move on until it is met.

## 10. Retention and Success Metrics

### 10.1 Retention strategy

A job app is used in bursts. A student who finds a regular job may not open it for weeks, and that is success, not churn. We therefore retain users by bringing them back at the moments they need work or staff, not by daily habits.

|**Side**|**Lever**|**How it works**|
|---|---|---|
|**Students**|Matching alerts|A push notification when a new post fits the student’s free slots and skills. This is the core reason to come back.|
|**Students**|Semester rhythm|A prompt to update the availability calendar at the start of each semester and before exams, when timetables change. Stale availability makes matching wrong.|
|**Students**|A CV that grows|Each completed shift is added to the work history and the CV. Leaving the app means leaving that record behind.|
|**Students**|No silent rejections|Employers are reminded to answer within 48 hours. Unanswered applications expire and the student is told, so the app never feels like a void.|
|**Students**|Early access|Premium students see new posts 6 hours before others, a reason to open the app early and to renew.|
|**Employers**|Fast first hire|Posting and accepting are free, and the team follows up personally with the first 50 employers in Algiers.|
|**Employers**|Post again|A finished post can be republished in one tap, and past workers can be invited again.|
|**Employers**|No-show visibility|Employers see each student’s no-show rate, and two no-shows suspend a student’s applications for 30 days (R8).|
|**Employers**|Bulk accounts|Agencies and event organisers get Employer Premium and a named contact in the team.|
|**Both**|Seasonal campaigns|Campaigns timed to demand peaks such as Ramadan evenings, summer and wedding season, and to supply dips during exams.|
|**Both**|Trust|Verified employers, ratings after each completed shift, and fast action on reports. Users who feel safe come back.|

### 10.2 Success metrics

We review these at every milestone. Each has a target and a reason, and the first one decides when Premium and advertising start. Daily active over monthly active users is left out on purpose: in an app used in bursts it would mark a satisfied student as a lost one.

|**Metric**|**Target**|**Why this number**|
|---|---|---|
|**Fill rate: posts with pay (unpaid events excluded) with an accepted student within 48 hours**|≥ 60%|The liquidity target. When it holds for four weeks in Algiers, Premium and advertising switch on (section 7). Below it, charging would push employers away.|
|**Median time to first applicant**|≤ 6 hours|Employers posting shifts need people the same day. Slower than this, they go back to Facebook groups.|
|**Employer response within 48 hours**|≥ 70% of applications|Students who are ignored stop applying, and supply collapses.|
|**Availability calendar completed**|≥ 80% of students|Matching depends on it. Below this, F3 needs a redesign or timetable import (5.3).|
|**Shifts completed per week**|Grows every month|The north-star metric. It is the value both sides came for, and the base of Premium revenue.|
|**Employers posting again within 60 days**|≥ 40%|Repeat employers carry the revenue. Below this, the problem is trust or quality, not acquisition.|
|**No-show rate on accepted shifts**|≤ 10%|Above this, employers stop trusting the students.|
|**Student Day-30 retention**|≥ 25%|Lower than a daily app on purpose, since use comes in bursts. Below it, matching alerts are not relevant enough.|
|**Employer Premium conversion**|≥ 5%|The assumption Years 1 and 2 rest on (7.3). Measured on the first cohort after Premium starts.|
|**Employer Premium monthly churn**|≤ 8%|Fixes the 12.5-month lifetime the CLV in 8.1 is built on. At 12% churn, the lifetime falls to 8.3 months.|
|**Student Premium conversion**|≥ 2%|The assumption behind Student Premium revenue (7.3), if it can be charged (R2).|
|**Reports per 100 posts**|≤ 2|Trust is a core promise against Facebook groups. Above this, verification moves from Should to Must.|
|**Crash-free sessions**|≥ 99%|Exit condition for the public beta. A crash during an application loses the shift.|

All targets are starting assumptions for a new market. The first beta cohort replaces them with measured values.
